Is It Legal to Pressure Wash Your Driveway in Indianapolis?

Pressure washing your own driveway is legal — the rules kick in around where the wastewater is allowed to go.

Short answer: yes, pressure washing your own driveway is legal in Indianapolis, and no permit is required for a homeowner cleaning their own property. The part people don't realize is regulated is what happens to the water afterward — and that part genuinely matters.

The Actual Rule: It's About the Runoff, Not the Washing

Under Indianapolis-Marion County's stormwater regulations, the only things legally allowed to enter a storm drain are rainwater, snowmelt, lawn irrigation runoff, and groundwater from sump pumps. Anything else — including wash water carrying detergent, oil, dirt, or other residue — is classified as an illicit discharge if it flows into a storm drain, ditch, or directly into a creek or waterway.

Why This Distinction Exists

Storm drains in Indianapolis flow directly to local waterways without treatment — unlike sanitary sewer water, which goes to a treatment plant. Detergent, oil residue from a driveway, and dirt-laden runoff sent straight into a storm drain becomes pollution in local creeks and rivers, which is the entire reason the rule exists. It's regulated under the city's Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) permit program.

What This Means in Practice for a Homeowner

For a simple water-only rinse of a driveway with minimal debris, the practical risk is low. But once you're using a cleaning solution, degreaser, or cleaning up something like an oil stain, that runoff shouldn't be allowed to flow freely into a street storm drain. Best practice — and what we do on every job — is containing and properly directing wastewater rather than letting it run downhill into the nearest drain.
