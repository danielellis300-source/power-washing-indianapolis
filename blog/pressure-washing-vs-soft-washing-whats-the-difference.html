Pressure Washing vs. Soft Washing: What's the Difference?

Two different processes, two different sets of surfaces — here’s how to tell which one your project actually needs.

"Pressure washing" gets used as a catch-all term, but it actually describes two distinct processes that use different equipment settings, different chemicals, and are suited to completely different surfaces. Using the wrong one is one of the most common (and expensive) DIY mistakes we see.

Pressure Washing: High Force, Hard Surfaces

Pressure washing uses concentrated high-pressure water — typically 3,000–4,000 PSI for professional equipment — to mechanically blast dirt, grime, and stains off durable surfaces. It's the right call for:

  • Concrete driveways and sidewalks
  • Brick patios and walkways
  • Some masonry and stone surfaces
  • Certain fencing materials (with caution)

The force does the work here. That's exactly why it's the wrong tool for softer surfaces — it doesn't discriminate between grime and the surface underneath it.

Soft Washing: Low Pressure, Chemistry Does the Work

Soft washing uses low pressure (often under 500 PSI, closer to a garden hose) combined with a specialized cleaning solution that kills algae, mold, mildew, and bacteria at the root. Instead of blasting growth off the surface, the solution breaks it down chemically, and a gentle rinse carries it away. This is the correct method for:

  • Roof shingles
  • Vinyl, wood, and fiber-cement siding
  • Stucco
  • Painted surfaces
  • Delicate landscaping-adjacent areas

Why Using the Wrong Method Causes Damage

High-pressure water on shingles can strip protective granules and shorten roof lifespan. On siding, it can force water behind panels and into wall cavities, leading to hidden moisture problems. On wood decking, it can gouge and splinter the surface. Meanwhile, using soft washing's low pressure on a heavily stained concrete driveway simply won't remove ground-in stains — it's the wrong tool in the other direction, just a less damaging mistake.

Why Soft-Washed Surfaces Stay Clean Longer

Because soft washing kills algae and mold spores rather than just knocking visible growth off the surface, soft-washed roofs and siding tend to stay clean noticeably longer than a surface that was simply blasted with water. This matters a lot in Indianapolis, where summer humidity gives algae and mildew ideal regrowth conditions — more in our guide to Indianapolis humidity and home exteriors.

How We Decide Which to Use

We match the method to the surface on every job — often using both in the same visit, soft washing the siding and roof while pressure washing the driveway and walkways. If you're getting quotes and a company plans to use the same equipment and pressure on your roof as your driveway, that's worth asking more questions about.
