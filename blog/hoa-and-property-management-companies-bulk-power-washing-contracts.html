HOA & Property Management Bulk Power Washing Contracts: What to Know

How bulk service contracts work for communities and portfolios, and what’s worth negotiating beyond just price.

For HOA boards and property management companies overseeing multiple units or a shared community, a one-off, per-request approach to power washing rarely makes sense at scale. Here's how bulk or recurring contracts typically work, and what's worth paying attention to beyond the headline price.

What Bulk Contracts Typically Cover

For HOAs, this often means common areas — clubhouse exteriors, entrance signage and monuments, shared sidewalks, pool decks, and sometimes mailbox kiosks — on a recurring schedule, sometimes paired with an optional resident discount program for individual home cleanings. For property management companies, it typically means a standing rate across all managed units, scheduled by season or by unit turnover rather than one-off requests.

Why Bulk Pricing Beats Individual Requests

Grouping properties or common areas into a single contract reduces scheduling overhead and travel time between jobs, which typically translates into a better effective rate than sourcing individual quotes for each property or area separately. It also means predictable, budgetable costs rather than variable one-off invoices throughout the year.

What to Negotiate Beyond Price

  • Scheduling flexibility: can service be timed around community events, or does it need to avoid certain days/times?
  • Response time for reactive requests: even with a recurring schedule, occasional out-of-cycle requests (a specific complaint, an incident) will come up
  • Documentation: for HOA boards fielding resident questions, having a clear service log helps demonstrate the community is being proactively maintained
  • Insurance and liability coverage: especially important at community scale where common-area work affects shared liability — see is your pressure washer insured?

Resident-Facing Benefits for HOAs

Some communities extend a group discount rate to individual homeowners who want their own property cleaned outside the common-area contract — a nice value-add for residents and a way to encourage more consistent community-wide upkeep without the HOA mandating individual spending. This pairs naturally with the appearance standards covered in HOA exterior cleaning rules.
