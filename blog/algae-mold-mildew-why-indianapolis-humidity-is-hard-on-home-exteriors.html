Algae, Mold & Mildew: Why Indianapolis Humidity Is Hard on Home Exteriors

The climate science behind why your Indianapolis home fights algae and mildew harder than homes in drier regions.

If it feels like your home's exterior gets dirty faster than homes you've seen in drier states, that's not your imagination — Indiana's humid continental climate genuinely creates better growing conditions for algae, mold, and mildew than most of the country.

Why Humidity Is the Key Factor

Algae, mold, and mildew all require sustained moisture to establish and spread — not just occasional rain, but consistent ambient humidity that keeps surfaces damp long after a rain event has passed. Indianapolis summers regularly see humidity levels that keep shaded, less-sun-exposed surfaces damp for extended stretches, which is exactly the environment these organisms need to thrive.

Why Shade Makes It Worse, Not Better

It's counterintuitive, but the shadiest, most protected parts of your home are usually the most affected — not the areas exposed to harsh weather. North-facing walls, areas under mature tree canopy, and spots that rarely get direct sun stay damp longer after rain and don't get the UV exposure that naturally inhibits algae growth on sun-exposed surfaces.

Why This Is Worse Than "Just Dirt"

Unlike ordinary dirt, algae and mildew are living organisms that actively grow and spread via airborne spores — which is why they often reappear within weeks of an inadequate cleaning, and why they frequently show up on multiple homes in the same neighborhood around the same time. Treating them like ordinary surface grime (a quick scrub or rinse) misses the biological root of the problem; see how to remove black streaks from vinyl siding for why this distinction matters practically.

Why Soft Washing Specifically Addresses This

Soft washing's cleaning solution is formulated to kill algae, mold, and mildew at the root rather than just remove the visible surface growth — which is exactly why it's the right approach for our climate specifically, not just a gentler alternative to pressure washing. What This Means for Maintenance Frequency

Because of our climate, an annual (rather than every-few-years) cleaning schedule genuinely makes sense for most Indianapolis homes — not as an upsell, but because algae and mildew have a real head start here compared to drier regions. Homes with heavy shade or dense tree cover, common throughout established neighborhoods in Zionsville and similar suburbs, sometimes benefit from an even tighter cycle.

Timing Cleaning Around the Climate

Since growth accelerates through the humid summer months, cleaning in spring — before peak growing season — tends to keep homes looking better for longer than a mid-summer or fall cleaning. See our full breakdown in best time of year to pressure wash your home.
