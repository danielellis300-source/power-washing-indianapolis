DIY Pressure Washing vs. Hiring a Professional: What's Really Cheaper?

The real math on DIY vs. professional pressure washing, once you count rental costs, your own time, and the risk of getting it wrong.

Renting a pressure washer for a weekend feels like the budget option, and sometimes it is. But the full comparison includes more than just the sticker price of hiring a pro versus a rental fee — here's the real math.

What DIY Actually Costs

  • Pressure washer rental: $60–$120/day for a consumer-grade unit
  • Surface cleaner attachment (if not included): $30–$60
  • Cleaning solution/detergent: $20–$40
  • Your time: a full day for most homes, often a full weekend for a house-plus-driveway job if you're learning as you go

Add it up and a DIY driveway cleaning can run $80–$150 in direct costs before counting your own time — not far off from professional pricing for the same job, without professional-grade equipment or results.

Where DIY Genuinely Makes Sense

Small, contained jobs on durable surfaces — a small patio, a single walkway, patio furniture — are reasonable DIY territory if you're comfortable with the equipment and take it slow. The risk profile is low, the surfaces are forgiving, and the time investment is manageable.

Where DIY Gets Expensive Fast

Roof cleaning, siding, and anything requiring ladder work are where DIY risk climbs sharply. Consumer pressure washers used on shingles or vinyl siding can cause real damage — stripped granules, cracked panels, water forced behind siding — that costs far more to repair than the job would have cost to hire out correctly the first time. The Uneven Results Problem

Consumer-grade equipment runs at lower, less consistent pressure than professional rigs, and without a surface cleaner attachment, DIY concrete cleaning often leaves visible "striping" where passes overlap unevenly. Getting a uniform, professional-looking result takes practice most weekend DIYers don't have the reps to build.

The Risk You Can't Rent Away

If something goes wrong — a cracked window, damaged siding, a slip off a ladder — there's no insurance policy covering a DIY mistake on your own property the way a professional company's liability coverage would. See is your pressure washer insured? for what that protection actually covers.

Our Honest Take

For small, low-risk surfaces, DIY is a reasonable choice. For roofs, siding, and anything requiring real technique to avoid damage, the cost difference between DIY and professional work is smaller than it looks once you count rental fees, your time, and the risk of a mistake.
